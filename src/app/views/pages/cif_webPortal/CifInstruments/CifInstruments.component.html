FAQs

Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FE SEM)
Q1: How many samples can be submitted in a single request form?
Ans: Maximum of 5 samples
Q2: Does the Gold sputtering process affect the sample?
Ans: No, the process only creates a very thin coating on the sample surface to enhance the conductivity for good imaging.
Q3: What amount of sample is required for FESEM?
Ans:
  • Powder: Minimum of 2.5 to 5mg.
  • Film: Maximum allowed size 1cm x 1cm.
Q4: How will we come to know about the slot allocation?
Ans: The concerned operator/office assistant will intimate you at least a week before your slot by calling you.
UV-Vis Spectrophotometer
Q1:What wavelength range is used for your UV-Vis instrument?
Ans: 200 nm to 800 nm
Q2: Can I reuse the sample after the analysis for other experiments?
Ans: Yes, if your sample is not UV sensitive you can reuse it. UV-Vis spectrometer analysis is a non-destructive method.
Q3: Is it necessary to provide final dilution of sample for UV analysis?
Ans:Yes, user must provide the sample in final diluted form.
Thermogravimetric analysis
Q1: How much sample is required for TGA analysis?
Ans: 5-8mg
Q2: What is the normally used heating rate in TGA analysis?
Ans: 10⁰ C/Min
Q3: Can we do TGA analysis of Liquid samples?
Ans: No! TGA analysis can only be performed on Powder or Film samples.
Q4: What is the maximum possible heating range of the TGA instrument available in CIF?
Ans: The maximum temperature range of our instrument is 1000 Degree Celsius.
Differential Scanning Calorimeter (DSC)
Q1: How much sample is required for DSC analysis?
Ans: 8 to 10mg
Q2: Which gases are used for the analysis process?
Ans: Nitrogen
Q3: Which type of sample pan is used in DSC?
Ans: Aluminium type
Powder X-ray Diffractometer (XRD)
Q1: Can I use the sample after XRD analysis?
Ans: Yes! XRD is a non-destructive analysis. So, the sample can be used again. We take utmost care not to contaminate the sample during analysis.
Q2:I have an unknown mineral sample. Can I use XRD to identify the sample?
Ans: Yes! XRD can be used to identify the crystal pattern or phases of the sample. For better results and proper identification, we recommend running XRF analysis prior to XRD to identify the elemental composition.
Q3: Which Databases are available in CSIF?
Ans:
  • ICSD (Inorganic Crystal Structure Database)
  • PDF2 ICDD (Previously JCPDS)
  • Software: Highscore Plus
Q4: What amount of sample is required for XRD?
Ans: Powder: Minimum of 400-500 mg
FTIR Spectrometer
Q1: What type of samples can be analyzed using FTIR Spectrometer?
Ans: Powder and Liquid samples.
Q2: Can we analyze liquid samples in FTIR Spectrometer?
Ans: Yes.
Q3: In which all ranges can we obtain IR spectra?
Ans:4000-400cm-1
Q4: How much sample would be required for FTIR Analysis?
Ans: 5mg - 10mg
Gas Chromatography coupled with Mass spectrometry and Flame Ionization Detector
Q1: Can gas samples can be analyzed using GCMS/MS in CIF?
Ans: No, samples in Solid and liquid form only can be analyzed in GCMS/MS.
Q2: Do I have to submit reference standards for Quantitative analysis?
Ans: Yes, for quantitative analysis user must provide the reference standards.
Q3: Does GCMS/MS results are provided with Library comparison?
Ans: Yes, NIST library comparison data is provided for each and every compound detected in samples.
Q4: Direct injection of Mass sample to get mass spectra is possible in CIF or not?
Ans: Yes, Direct mass spectra can be obtained by using MS/MS in CIF Facility.
High Performance Liquid Chromatography
Q1: What is the minimum quantity required for HPLC analysis?
Ans:
  • For powder samples - 5-10 mg
  • For powder samples - Minimum 2 ml of final Dilution.
Q2: Do I have to submit reference standards for Quantitative analysis?
Ans: Yes, for quantitative analysis user must provide the reference standards.
Q3: What kind of detectors are available in HPLC?
Ans: Two detectors are available in HPLC
  • Photo Diode Array (PDA)
  • Refrective index Detector (RID)
Particle size analyser
Q1: What are some of the specifications of the instrument?
Ans: Zetasizer nano uses a laser with λ=633 nm. Samples that absorb in this region are not suitable for analysis. The instrument has a temperature controller capable at examining samples from 2⁰ C-90⁰ C.
Q2: What cuvettes are available for the experiments?
Ans: Low volume quartz cuvette is available for check out. Disposable polystyrene cuvettes are available at the instrument. “Disposable” multiuse folded capillary polystyrene cuvettes for size/zeta measurements are located by the instrument
Q3:What is the volume of the sample needed for measurements?
Ans:Disposable polystyrene cuvettes require 1 mL of a sample, folded capillary cells take 0.75 mL. The minimum volume of a sample for low volume quartz cell is 12 μL.
Q4:What is a good concentration for my sample?
Ans:It depends on the optical properties of the particles, the particle size and polydispersity of the sample.
Fluorescence
Q1: What type of samples can be analyzed using Fluorescence Spectrometer?
Ans: Powder and Liquid samples
Q2:How much sample would be required for FTIR Analysis?
Ans: For powder samples minimum 200 mg of sample is required. For liquid samples minimum 2 ml of sample is required.
Q3:Do I need to provide excitation and emission ranges for analysis?
Ans: Yes, user must provide all the specifications required for analysis.
Q4:What if you do not know the wavelength region in which your sample will show fluorescence?
Ans: Go for UV spectroscopy measurement, you will get the fluorescence where UV spectrum shows.
ICP-OES
Q1: For elemental analysis, should I submit the final dilution of prepared sample?
Ans: Yes, only final dilution of sample with digestion should be submitted for analysis.
Q2:What is the minimum quantity of sample required for analysis in ICP-OES?
Ans: 40-50 ml of final dilution of sample is required for analysis.
Q3:Do I need to provide reference standards for quantitative analysis?
Ans:Yes, user has to provide reference standards for quantitative estimation.
Electrochemical Workstation
Q1: What are the possible area in which electrochemical measurements are being offered?
Ans: Supercapacitors, Batteries , Biosensors, Corrosions, Electrodepositions
Q2: Are you providing photo sensitive measurements of area like photo-detectors, solar cell, photo-catalyst etc. by electrochemical workstation?
Ans: No, as we do not have light source availability right now.
Q3: Which type of electrodes are being provided for electrochemical measurements?
Ans: Ag/AgCl as reference electrode, Platinum wire as counter electrode, Glass carbon electrode and carbon electrodes.
Q4: Do we need to provide the measurement’s parameter?
Ans:Yes, user has to provide all the details of parameter for doing the measurements by using electrochemical workstation
Q5:Do you prepare the working electrode for doing the electrochemical workstation?
Ans: User has to provide the fully prepared electrodes for doing the measurements. But in some cases electrode may be prepared by our team and extra charges will be applicable depending on the case to case.
Q6: Can I reuse the sample after the analysis for other experiments?
Ans:It depends on case to case do need to discuss with the scientific operator.
Viscometer
Q1: Can I analyse my solid samples in Viscometer?
Ans: No, user has to provide only liquid samples either free-flowing or slightly viscous samples
Q2: Can I analyse my liquid samples at higher temperature?
Ans: No, samples can be measured at only ambient temperature.
Q3:How much quantity of liquid samples is required to do the analysis?
Ans: At least 40-50 ml of liquid sample is required to analyse the viscosity of samples.
